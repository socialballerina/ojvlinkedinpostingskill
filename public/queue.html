China installed 295,000 industrial robots in 2024. The rest of the world managed 247,000 between them.

You can subsidise a factory. You cannot subsidise a habit.

Read from a desk in London, that number looks like industrial policy, and the comfortable conclusion is that it reverts when the subsidy stops.

Here is the part that does not revert. China's operational robot stock passed 2 million units in 2024, the largest of any country, and Chinese manufacturers now hold 57% of their own domestic robot market.

That second number is the one to sit with. The buyers stopped importing the tools.

On the ground this shows up as a different kind of conversation. Ask a plant manager in the Pearl River Delta what they automated last and you rarely get a strategy answer. You get a payback period, in months, and the name of a supplier two hours away who will modify the cell for you.

The ecosystem is the product, not the company. A robot you can get serviced on Thursday beats a better robot you cannot.

Recheck two figuresThe 1,000-products and 30%-sourcing figures came from a page that blocked direct fetch. The two headline claims are solid.
The post1303 chars · band 900 to 1300
India is supposed to be where Western retail goes to die. IKEA's answer was to rebuild the price, not the marketing.

When it opened its first Indian store in 2018, the range carried 1,000 products under 200 rupees and 500 under 100 rupees.

That was not a launch promotion. It was a supply chain decision.

Indian rules require a foreign single-brand retailer to source 30% of its materials locally within five years. IKEA treated the requirement as the strategy rather than the obstacle. It moved local sourcing from single digits to 20 to 25%, and by 2020 was saying that sourcing wood locally, including sheesham and bamboo, would let it cut prices by a third. More than a third of the products under those lower prices were made in India.

Most Western teams run this in the opposite order. Land at the home-market price, discover the buyer will not pay it, discount into a margin hole, then conclude the market is difficult.

Price has to be rebuilt from the local cost base up, before launch, not defended after it. That is a large part of what our GTM advisory work actually is.
