# OJV Brand Voice

Three parts: the language OJV already uses in public, what the best cross-border accounts
actually do on LinkedIn, and the house style rules. Read all three before drafting.

Research pass run 3 September 2026 by reading live posts, not by assumption. Each account's
rules below are drawn from posts observed on that date, quoted where useful.

## Part 2. What the researched accounts actually do

### 2a. Ashley Dudarenok (personal brand, China innovation tours)
Headline observed: "China Innovation Research & Foresights | China Learning Expeditions |
Innovation Tours | China Study Tours for Corporates | Keynote Speaker | Author | LinkedIn Top Voice"

| Dimension | What she does | Adopt for OJV? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | One line, concrete claim or number, one emoji. "This robot jumps 2m and beats Bolt.🤖" / "Two weeks in Shenzhen will rewire your brain. 🧠" / "The truth about the US robot ban! 🤖" | Adopt the shape. Drop the emoji from the hook. |
| Line 2 | An aphorism that reframes the news. "You can ban a product overnight, but you can't ban a supply chain." | **Adopt.** This is the single most transferable move in the research. |
| Length | Short. 400 to 700 characters. | Partially. OJV runs 900 to 1,300 because the archetypes carry an argument. |
| Rhythm | Every sentence its own paragraph. Blank line between all of them. | Adopt. |

| Links | Never in the body. "Link below" or "DM me to apply". | Adopt the mechanic. LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with outbound links, so first comment or DM keyword. |
| Hashtags | None observed. | OJV uses 3 to 5 at the end. Keep it low, not zero. |
| Offer ratio | Very high. 4 of 5 posts observed pitched the tour. | **Do not adopt.** A personal brand can pitch every day. A company page doing it reads as an ad account. OJV holds 2 value : 1 offer. |

### 2c. Company-page benchmarks

**Antler** (tagline "Go Further, Faster.", 363K followers). Third-person for its own people
("Shambhavi Mishra sits down with Mike Kim"), first-person plural for the firm. Short punchy hook
on video posts: "You can't fake compliance." Podcast and portfolio content dominates. One link,
in body, via `lnkd.in`, prefixed `👉`. No hashtags observed. **Uses em dashes: do not copy.**

**SOSV** (tagline "SOSV is the most active deep tech venture capital firm globally", 48K
followers). First person plural throughout: "our friends in Australia", "our work in critical
materials recovery", "Congrats to our founders!". Emoji-prefixed lists for portfolio roll-calls.
Reposts third-party trade press with a framing comment and credits the outlet by name ("More
details below in Mining Magazine"). **Puts hashtags inline mid-sentence, which breaks the read:
do not copy.** OJV puts them at the end.

**Techstars** (tagline "Techstars helps founders scale their startups into world-changing
businesses.", 364K followers). `→` as the list bullet, three items. Named-executive quote block
inside partnership announcements. Links pushed to the comments: "🎧 Link in comments." / "Use the
link in comments to learn more. 👇". **Opens announcements with "We are proud to announce": do not
copy**, it is the same move as the banned "thrilled to announce".

**Entrepreneur First** was in the research plan but its LinkedIn page would not load during this
pass, so no rules were drawn from it. Do not cite EF conventions until that gap is filled.

### 2d. What a company page must not copy from a personal brand

1. **Pitching in every post.** Both personal accounts sell constantly. A personal brand is a
   person you follow; a page that sells every time gets muted. Hold 2 value : 1 offer.
2. **"DM me".** A page cannot receive a personal DM the way a person can. Route DM keywords to a
   named partner's inbox, or use the enquiry link.
3. **First-person singular and personal anecdote.** "I've seen this multiple times during 15
   years operating inside Chinese companies" is Sean's strongest asset and is unavailable to a
   page. If a draft needs lived experience to work, publish it from the partner's profile and
   reshare from the page. See Part 4.
4. **Unsourced numbers.** Both accounts get away with it on personal credibility. A page does not.
5. **Emoji density.** Ashley's emoji-per-line rhythm reads as personality on a face account and
   as clip-art on a company page. Cap at two emoji per post, none in the hook.
6. **Hype adjectives about oneself.** OJV's own page has done this. Fix it, do not repeat it.

## Part 3. Style rules

1. **Hook lands in the first 140 characters**, before LinkedIn truncates. Count it.
2. **900 to 1,300 characters** for standard posts. Behind-the-scenes posts may run shorter.
3. **One idea per line.** Generous line breaks. No walls of text.
4. **Three to five hashtags maximum**, at the end, never inline.
5. **First person plural** for the company page. "We", "our". Never "OJV believes" in the third
   person on its own page.
6. **No em dashes anywhere.** Use a comma, a full stop, or restructure.
7. **Banned openers and phrases:** "thrilled to announce", "excited to announce", "proud to
   announce", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "revolutionizing", "underscores our
   dedication", "stay tuned", "we look forward to supporting".
8. **Every number carries a source link.** If we cannot source it, we cut it.
9. **Close with a question or a CTA, never both.**

Supporting mechanics: outbound links go in the first comment or via a DM keyword, not the body,
except where a post's whole value is the link. Every outbound link is UTM tagged. Numbers get the
comparative form ("6% against 65%") rather than the bare form where the source supports it.

### Hook patterns that tested well in the research

| Pattern | Template | Example shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reframe | `<Thing you can do>, but <thing you cannot>.` | "You can ban a product overnight, but you can't ban a supply chain." |
| By design | `<Bad situation>. Not by accident, but by design.` | "Your China and global teams don't get along. Not by accident, but by design." |
| Contrarian bet | `Would you bet against <X> because <comfortable assumption>?` | The GoPro hook. |
| Local claim broken | `<Place> is supposed to <normal state>. <Number that breaks it>.` | The August in Hong Kong hook. |
| Number first | `<Specific number>. <What it means>.` | "542% on day one. That is not a robotics story." |

Do not run the same pattern twice in a fortnight. Log which pattern each post used in the
calendar `hook` field so the monthly review can catch fatigue.

## Part 4. Company page or personal profile

Some posts are simply better from a person. Route the draft to Naman's profile, and reshare from
the page, when any of these is true:

- The post needs first-person lived experience ("I sat in that factory").
- The post takes a position that would read as a corporate stance if the page said it.
- The post is about a rejection, a failure, or a mistake, including OJV's own.
- The post asks the reader for something personal, such as a DM.
- The post names and praises a specific individual.

Keep the page for: market insight with sources, portfolio and partnership news, event and trek
offers, and behind-the-scenes with partners' faces.

When a draft is routed to a personal profile, the draft file must say so in its front matter
(`voice: naman-personal`), rewrite it in first person singular, and note in the run summary that
the page's job is the reshare.
