Asics' lifestyle sneakers grew 77% in Europe in the first half of 2026. Its running shoes grew 10%.

Same company. A lot of the same shoes. A different buyer.

The product was never the problem. Performance running grew in every region Asics sells in.

What changed was who it is for. A runner buys on cushioning and mileage. The SportStyle buyer buys on the archive, the colourway, and who else is wearing it.

So Asics rebuilt the other two things.

The price. Its own filing says the range is "shifting toward a more premium direction, with a focus on products priced above USD100."

The story. Models from the '90s and '00s, restructured as archive lines, and collaborations with designers such as Kiko Kostadinov.

Across Europe, sales were up 29.7% on a currency-neutral basis, with SportStyle leading.

This is the pattern in most Asia-to-West moves we see. The product rarely needs to change. The buyer is a different person, the price has to be rebuilt for how they buy, and the story has to be written for them.
