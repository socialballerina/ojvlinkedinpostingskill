## Copy

In 2005 a Chinese PC maker put its executive headquarters in Purchase, New York. Not by accident, but by design.

Lenovo did not need to learn how to build a computer. What it did not have was the Western buyer.

Enterprise IT buys from a name it already trusts, through a rep it already knows, with financing and service it already has contracts for.

So Lenovo bought IBM's PC business, in a deal completed on 1 May 2005, for US$1.25 billion: about US$650 million in cash and US$600 million in Lenovo shares.

Lenovo's PCs would be marketed through IBM's worldwide distribution and sales network. IBM became its preferred supplier of services and financing. IBM ended up owning 18.9% of Lenovo, so the old seller had a reason to keep selling.

The buyer, the financing and the story came in one deal, not built from zero.

Most Asian companies moving West spend year one proving the product. The product is rarely what is missing. The buyer is a different person, and the story has to land in a room that never saw your home-market win.

You do not need a billion dollars to apply this. You need to know which relationship your buyer already trusts, and route through it. That is the first thing our GTM advisory work maps.

We were not involved in Lenovo's deal. This is commentary.
