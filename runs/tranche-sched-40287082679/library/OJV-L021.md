## Copy

Hesai's lidar shipments rose 78.4% in the second quarter of 2026. Its revenue rose 21.9%.

That gap is the story, and it is not a weak quarter.

Hesai shipped 628,275 lidar units in those three months. 142,371 of them were for robotics rather than cars, up 193.4% on a year earlier. Net revenue was RMB860.8 million, and the quarter was profitable.

Read from a desk in London or San Francisco, units racing ahead of revenue looks like a price war, and the comfortable conclusion is to wait for it to burn out.

That reading gets the fact right and the meaning wrong. A sensor that gets cheaper every quarter stops being only a car part and starts being a robot part. That is what the robotics line shows. The cost curve moved, and a new set of buyers appeared underneath it.

A results table cannot show you that loop. In China's hardware clusters the robot makers buying these sensors sit close to the people making them, and a cheaper part turns into a new product faster than a Western roadmap expects.

The ecosystem is the product, not the company. Falling prices are how a component finds its next market.
