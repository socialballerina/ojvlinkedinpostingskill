Nobody wants to hear this, but China's energy-storage boom came with a warning from its own regulator.

In October 2026 China's industry ministry said energy-storage battery output exceeded 420 gigawatt-hours in the first half of 2026, up 110% on a year earlier. Overseas orders signed in those six months reached 298 GWh, up 83%.

In the same speech the vice minister called on the industry to resolutely prevent "involution-style" competition. That is the official phrase for price wars and overcapacity.

Read from Europe, the reflex is to pick one half. Either China is about to own grid storage, or it is a bubble about to pop. Both are true at once, and the useful question is which suppliers come out the other side.

A production statistic cannot answer that. It gets answered in conversations with the cell makers, the system integrators and the buyers behind those overseas orders, by working out who is pricing for share and who is pricing to last.
