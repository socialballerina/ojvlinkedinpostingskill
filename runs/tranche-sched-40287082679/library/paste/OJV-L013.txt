179,877. That is how many cars BYD shipped outside China in September 2026, up 153.9% on a year earlier.

The monthly number is not the news. The share is.

In the first nine months of 2026 BYD sold 3,131,576 vehicles. 1,337,831 of them were overseas shipments of passenger cars and pickups. Roughly four in every ten.

Read from a desk in Frankfurt or Detroit, the comfortable conclusion is that this is a subsidy wave that tariffs will stop. That reading misses where the advantage sits.

China as a whole exported 8.205 million vehicles in those same nine months, more than 4 million of them new energy vehicles. One company did not build that. A supply base did: cells, motors, castings and software suppliers who have spent years being priced against each other at home before any of them looked abroad.

That is the part you cannot see in a sales disclosure. On the ground in the Pearl River Delta, the question a parts supplier asks is rarely whether you can afford them. It is how fast you need the revision.

The ecosystem is the product, not the company. Tariffs move a price. They do not move a supply base.
