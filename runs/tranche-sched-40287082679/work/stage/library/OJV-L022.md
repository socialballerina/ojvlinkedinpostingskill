## Copy

1,975 robotaxis. That was the global fleet Pony.ai reported in its second-quarter 2026 results.

The fleet is not the news. The fares are.

In that quarter Pony.ai's robotaxi revenue reached US$12.1 million, up 691.2% on a year earlier, and fare-charging revenue rose 849.3%. Total revenue was US$36.2 million, up 68.8%, while the net loss narrowed 14.9% to US$45.4 million.

Read from a desk in New York or Munich, driverless ride-hailing still looks like a science project that will burn cash for another decade. These numbers describe something else: a paid service, still loss-making, growing far faster than its losses.

The reason is not one clever model. A robotaxi fleet is a hardware problem as much as a software one. Every new car needs sensors, compute and a vehicle to put them in, and in China the suppliers for all three already build at electric-vehicle volumes.

That is the part you cannot read in an earnings release. The software gets the headlines. The speed comes from how close the parts are.

The ecosystem is the product, not the company.
