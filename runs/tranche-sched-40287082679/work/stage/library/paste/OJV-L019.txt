China was supposed to make every foreign carmaker take a local partner. In July 2018 Tesla became the first that did not.

Beijing had promised that April to end the rule requiring foreign automakers to work through local partners. Tesla moved within three months: its first factory outside the US, in Shanghai, as the first wholly foreign-owned automaker in China.

Less than 12 months after breaking ground, Tesla said at the start of 2020 that the plant had already produced just under 1,000 customer salable cars.

The unlock was regulatory sequencing. Tesla did not wait to see how the new rule would work in practice. It was first through the door the rule opened.

Then the part a 2018 post would have missed. In 2025 the Shanghai plant delivered 851,000 cars, more than half of Tesla's global deliveries. Over the same year, Tesla's retail sales inside China fell 4.78% to 625,698.

Both are true. Sequencing bought Tesla a structure no foreign rival had. It did not exempt the company from the market that structure sits in.
