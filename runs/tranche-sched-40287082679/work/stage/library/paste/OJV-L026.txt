Uber's way into Japan was to stop being Uber.

In 2015 Japanese authorities ordered Uber to halt its ridesharing pilot in Fukuoka, citing legal and regulatory issues.

In December 2020 Uber Japan signed a partnership with the taxi operator Hinomaru Kotsu, and the two agreed to oppose illegal ridesharing. From then on, Uber in Japan meant licensed taxis booked through the app.

By the end of 2025 Uber Taxi ran in all 47 prefectures, two years ahead of its 2027 target, with more than 1,000 partner taxi companies. That is according to the head of Uber Japan, who said in a February 2026 interview that it had grown at roughly double each year for about three years.

The unlock was regulatory sequencing. Uber did not fight the licensing system. It plugged into it. The drivers, cars and licences stayed with local operators, and Uber brought the demand.

In plenty of APAC's 15+ ecosystems the regulation is the market structure. The entrant that works through local licences gets to scale. The one that argues with them gets a pilot.
