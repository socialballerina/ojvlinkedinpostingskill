In May 2018 Walmart agreed to pay about $16 billion for roughly 77% of Flipkart. Here is what that bought that building could not:

Walmart did not launch its own Indian marketplace. It bought an Indian company that already had the customers, the sellers and the team.

→ The team stayed. Walmart's announcement said Flipkart's leadership would be supported by Walmart, alongside Tencent, Tiger Global and Microsoft.

→ The money went into the business too. The deal included $2 billion of new equity funding.

→ The company moved home. In March 2026 Flipkart completed its redomiciliation to India.

This is not a victory lap. In the year to March 2026 Flipkart's revenue rose 18% to Rs 1.46 lakh crore, and it still made a consolidated net loss of Rs 8,468 crore. In May 2026 it put its planned IPO on hold.

What the deal shows is a choice. In a market that size and that local, Walmart decided time was worth more than building from zero.
