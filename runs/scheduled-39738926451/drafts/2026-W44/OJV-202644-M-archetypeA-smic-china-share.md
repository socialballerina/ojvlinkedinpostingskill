## Copy

90.2%. That is the share of SMIC's second-quarter revenue that came from China. A year earlier it was 84.1%.

That is not a chip story. It is a customer story.

Read from a desk in London or New York, the headline was export controls. Cut a foundry off from Western tools and Western customers, and it shrinks.

The filing says otherwise. Revenue was $3,005.6 million in the quarter, up 36.1% on a year earlier. Utilisation was 93.7%.

America fell from 12.9% of revenue to 8.2%. The business grew anyway.

The customers who replaced that revenue are not named in a results table. The mix gives a hint: industrial and automotive went from 10.6% of wafer revenue to 16.5% in a year.

That is the part you cannot read from outside. Who the domestic chip designers filling those lines are, what they are building for carmakers and factories, and how quickly they move from a design to an order.

Those answers sit in meeting rooms in China, not in a filing.

When a market loses its foreign customers and keeps growing, the demand did not disappear. It moved somewhere you are not looking.
