## Copy

Britain is supposed to be a hard market for a car brand nobody has heard of. Two Chinese brands passed 100,000 sales there this summer.

Omoda arrived at the end of August 2024. Jaecoo followed at the start of 2025. The 100,000 mark came in less than two years, at a combined share of nearly 5.7% of new registrations.

Nobody in Britain was waiting for these names. So the hard part was never the car. It was the buyer, and the route to that buyer.

The buyer was a household, not a fleet desk. Of the more than 36,500 Omoda 5s sold, 71% went to private retail customers.

The route was built early: 144 dealerships across the country. Their UK chief executive said it plainly. Getting there "would require investing heavily in our people, our retailer network and the ownership experience."

That is the pattern in most Asia-to-West moves we see. A new name does not earn trust from an advert. It earns it from a showroom down the road and someone to call when something goes wrong.

The buyer, the route to them, and the story they hear first. Rebuilding those three is what our GTM advisory work is for.
