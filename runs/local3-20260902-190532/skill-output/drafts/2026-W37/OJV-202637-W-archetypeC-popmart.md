## Copy

Would you write off Pop Mart's US business because one month of card-spend data looked ugly?

In the first half of 2025, its US revenue grew 1,142% year on year, to $315 million. The Labubu blind boxes were real demand.

Then in March 2026, US sales fell 45% year on year on card-transaction data. The online spike cooled about as fast as it built.

What Pop Mart did next reads less like a retreat. It kept signing mall leases. A partnership with Simon Property Group is pushing its US and Canada store count past 60, with more than 20 new mall stores announced.

Its own half-year numbers this August back that up: total revenue up 23.8%, with what the company calls "steady expansion of offline channels" even as overseas growth cooled.

The viral spike got Pop Mart noticed. It did not build repeat customers. That takes a place someone can walk into on a Saturday, a retail relationship, not a moment.

Rebuilding a hype cycle into a habit is most of what our GTM advisory work is for.
