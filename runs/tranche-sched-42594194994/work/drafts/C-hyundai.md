## Copy

American buyers were supposed to be finished with Hyundai by 1998. In 1999 its US sales rose 82%.

The reputation problem was real. When Hyundai first came to the US, NPR later wrote, the name was synonymous with poor quality. It became a punch line on late-night talk shows.

By 1998 its US sales were heading for their lowest level ever, about 90,000.

In September 1998 Hyundai Motor America changed what the buyer was being asked to believe.

→ A 10-year, 100,000-mile powertrain warranty, plus five years and 60,000 miles bumper to bumper.
→ No deductibles on any of it.
→ A name for it: America's Best Warranty.

Sales in 1999 came to 164,190, against 90,217 the year before, and the company named the warranty as one factor bringing buyers into showrooms. In 2025 Hyundai sold 901,686 vehicles in the US, a record for the third year running.

The buyer did not believe the product. The warranty moved the cost of that doubt from the buyer to the brand.

That is the pattern in most Asia-to-West moves: the buyer, the pricing and the story, rarely the product. Rebuilding those is what our GTM advisory is for.

We were not involved in Hyundai's expansion. This is commentary.
