## Copy

In 2016 KFC and Pizza Hut had a little over 7,000 restaurants in China between them. By September 2026 KFC alone had 14,307.

The fried chicken did not do that. The ownership did.

In October 2016 Yum! Brands spun its China business off into a separate company, Yum China, listed in New York, with Primavera Capital and Ant Financial putting in $460 million.

From then on Yum China was the franchisee, a separate company built around one market, setting its own pace.

→ Pace. 1,706 net new stores in 2025, then 692 in the third quarter of 2026, a quarterly record.
→ Channel. Delivery was around 48% of company sales in 2025, up from 39% a year earlier.
→ Partners. By September 2026, 19% of stores were run by franchisees.

Not every number rose. Same-store sales fell 3% in that quarter. The store count kept climbing anyway, to 19,989.

The unlock was structure. What Yum! Brands did not do was keep China as one line on a global plan.

Who owns the market is a go-to-market decision before it is a legal one, and it is usually the first thing we test in GTM advisory.

We have never worked with Yum! Brands or Yum China. This is commentary from the outside.
