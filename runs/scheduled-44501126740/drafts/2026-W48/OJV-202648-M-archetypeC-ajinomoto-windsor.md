## Copy

Nobody wants to hear this, but in 2014 the top share of Asian frozen food in America belonged to an American company.

It was Windsor Quality. That September, Ajinomoto, the Japanese food group, agreed to buy it for about US$800 million.

Ajinomoto was already selling frozen food in North America, about US$130 million of it.

What it did not have was the shelf. Windsor's products were sold in about 80,000 US stores and bought by about 120,000 restaurants.

So Ajinomoto bought the American brands that already held the top share for Asian products, kept Windsor's management in place, and planned to push its own gyoza through Windsor's distribution.

The hard part was never the dumpling. It was the name on the bag, the shelf it sat on, and who the American buyer already trusted.

Asian companies moving West tend to assume the authentic product wins on its own. Sometimes it does. More often the shelf, the brand and the price point already belong to someone who understood that buyer first.

The useful question is much smaller than an acquisition. Who already owns your buyer's trust, and can you route through them before you spend on a launch?

We were not involved in this deal. This is commentary.
