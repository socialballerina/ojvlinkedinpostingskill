India is supposed to be where European carmakers stay small. Škoda's deliveries there almost doubled in 2025, to 70,600.

That made India Škoda's fourth-largest market, behind Germany, the Czech Republic and the UK.

The car that did it was not a European car with Indian paperwork.

The Kylaq went into series production in Pune in December 2024. It sits in India's sub-4-metre segment, which Škoda says accounts for nearly 50% of car sales in the country. Its platform was a joint development between engineers in the Czech Republic and India.

Škoda's chief executive put the 2025 growth down primarily to the Kylaq.

The unlock was building for the size class where the market actually buys, not shipping the European range and waiting for demand to arrive.
