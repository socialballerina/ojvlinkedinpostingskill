## Copy

Starbucks bought control of its China business, then sold it again. Not by accident, but by design.

It entered East China with regional partners, Uni-President Enterprises and President Chain Store, and let them build it.

In July 2017 it paid around $1.3 billion in cash for the remaining half of that business, the largest acquisition in its history at the time, taking full ownership of roughly 2,800 stores in China. In the same transaction it handed those partners its 50% of Taiwan for about $175 million.

In November 2025 it went the other way. Boyu Capital took up to 60% of the China retail business for $4 billion, Starbucks kept 40% and the brand licence, and the stated plan is to go from 8,000 stores to 20,000.

Ownership was never the goal. It was a dial.

Partner in to learn a market. Buy in when control is the scarce thing. Sell control when a local operator moves faster than you can and speed becomes the scarce thing.

APAC is not one market. It is 15+ ecosystems with different rules and different buyers, and the ownership structure is one of the things you recalibrate.
