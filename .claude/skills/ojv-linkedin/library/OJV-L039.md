## Copy

Too many corporate study trips to China come home with a photo album and no decision. Not by accident, but by design.

Give us an hour with a strategy team planning one and we can usually tell which kind of trip it will be.

We start with four things:

→ The question
→ The visits
→ Who travels
→ The flight home

Then we look at what is underneath them.

Is there one decision this trip is meant to inform, written down before anyone books a flight?

Were the visits picked to answer that question, or because they were the famous names that said yes?

Is the person who will own the decision on the trip, or reading a summary of it a month later?

Who writes up what the team saw, and by when, before the urgency wears off?

From the inside, a trip that changed nothing looks like a trip to the wrong places. Sometimes it was. More often it left without a question, and no visit can answer a question nobody asked.

That is the part of OJV Educate we care about most. The week is built backwards from the decision.

If you are planning an executive trip to China and need it to end in a decision, not a deck, work with us.

DM us, or write to us at hello@oj.ventures

## Photo brief

PICK: one of the co-founders with two or three executives around a table or standing at a
whiteboard, notebooks open, mid-discussion. Working posture, not a tour group pose. Landscape.
AVOID: a factory floor, showroom or named company's premises, which would imply a visit the copy
does not claim. Any legible client or company name on a screen or whiteboard. Faces of anyone who
is not a co-founder or has not cleared it. A trek group photo, which turns this into a trek post.
FALLBACK: text-only is acceptable.

WHY THIS BRIEF: the post sells the thinking before and after the trip, not the trip itself, so
the image should look like a working session, not like sightseeing.
