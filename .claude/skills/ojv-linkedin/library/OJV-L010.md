## Copy

Western manufacturers say humanoid robots are years from being useful. Their Chinese counterparts have been signing purchase orders:

BYD. Foxconn. Geely. FAW-Volkswagen Qingdao. Audi FAW. BAIC New Energy. Dongfeng Liuzhou. SF Express.

By November 2025, UBTECH reported accumulated orders above 800 million yuan, roughly $112 million, for its Walker S2 industrial humanoid. A single contract for a data collection centre in Zigong was worth 159 million yuan. The company said it was targeting annual capacity of 5,000 units in 2026, rising to 10,000 by 2027.

The tempting argument is whether the robots are any good yet. That is the wrong argument.

What an order book of that shape tells you is narrower and more useful. A set of large manufacturers has decided the technology is far enough along to put on a purchase order, and the supplier can build and service them inside the same manufacturing region as the buyer.

Capability gets debated at conferences. Purchase orders get delivered on a date.
