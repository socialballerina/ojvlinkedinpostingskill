Nobody wants to hear this, but Kikkoman won American kitchens by telling people to put soy sauce on meat.

In 1957 it set up a marketing company in San Francisco to break into the US market properly. The soy sauce was already the soy sauce. What changed was the story around it.

→ The label. In 1957 export bottles started carrying the words "ALL-PURPOSE SEASONING".
→ The pitch. A catchphrase, "Delicious on Meat", backed by in-store demonstrations at supermarkets.
→ The product. Teriyaki Sauce, sold from 1961, was launched in the US as a barbecue sauce.

Then the supply chain followed the demand. In 1973 full-scale production began at a plant in Walworth, Wisconsin. Kikkoman says its soy sauce is now made in nine overseas plants and sold in over 100 countries.

It did not ask American shoppers to learn Japanese cooking first. It walked into the dish they already made, in the aisle they already shopped.

That is the hard part of most Asia-to-West moves. Rarely the product. Usually the buyer, the pricing and the story, and the story most of all.
