80,000. That is roughly how many US stores carried Bibigo's Korean range by August 2026.

The dumplings were never the hard part. Getting them into American freezers was.

In November 2018 CJ CheilJedang agreed to buy 80% of Schwan's Company, a US frozen-food maker founded in 1952, for $1.84 billion. CJ named what it was buying: logistics, distribution and sales networks throughout the entire US.

It did not try to build American grocery distribution for a Korean brand one buyer at a time. It bought a company whose brands, Red Baron and Tony's pizza among them, were already in those aisles.

What followed, per the Korea Herald in August 2026:

→ Bibigo dumplings led the US dumpling market with a 41% share in 2024.
→ CJ's US food revenue rose from 3.32 trillion won in 2020 to 4.91 trillion won in 2025.
→ The US made up 83% of its overseas revenue.

The buyer for a frozen dumpling in America is not a fan of Korean food. It is a category buyer deciding what earns a freezer door, and a distributor deciding what is worth a truck.

That is usually where an Asian brand stalls in the West: the route to the buyer.
