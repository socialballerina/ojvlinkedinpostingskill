Nobody wants to hear this, but Netflix grew faster in India by charging less.

In December 2021 it cut its Indian prices by 18 to 60% across plans. Mobile went from Rs 199 a month to Rs 149. Basic went from Rs 499 to Rs 199.

In its April 2023 shareholder letter, Netflix said the cuts, with a stronger slate, helped grow engagement in India by nearly 30%, while F/X neutral revenue growth in 2022 accelerated to 24%, from 19% in 2021.

Then came the part that shows it was a strategy, not a promotion. In the first quarter of 2023 it cut prices in another 116 countries, using what India had taught it.

The unlock was a pricing rebuild. What Netflix did not do was defend one global price and decide the market was too poor to pay it.

Most Western subscription teams convert the home price into local currency and call that localisation. Conversion stays low, and the conclusion is that the market is not ready.

Price is a market-entry decision, not a finance one. Rebuilt for how that buyer actually pays, it can be the whole unlock. It is one of the first things we test in our GTM advisory work.
