Too many corporate study trips to China come home with a photo album and no decision. Not by accident, but by design.

Give us an hour with a strategy team planning one and we can usually tell which kind of trip it will be.

We start with four things:

→ The question
→ The visits
→ Who travels
→ The flight home

Then we look at what is underneath them.

Is there one decision this trip is meant to inform, written down before anyone books a flight?

Were the visits picked to answer that question, or because they were the famous names that said yes?

Is the person who will own the decision on the trip, or reading a summary of it a month later?

Who writes up what the team saw, and by when, before the urgency wears off?

From the inside, a trip that changed nothing looks like a trip to the wrong places. Sometimes it was. More often it left without a question, and no visit can answer a question nobody asked.
