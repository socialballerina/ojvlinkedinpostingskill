In June 2008 you could have bought 10% of Airbnb for $150,000. Seven investors were asked and none of them did.

Two things are worth unpacking, and only one is about the investors.

Brian Chesky was raising $150,000 at a $1.5 million valuation. Of the seven he approached, five passed and two never replied at all. He published the rejection emails himself, with the names removed.

The reasons were not insults. They were mandate. Not in our area of focus. Not the right opportunity. A long shot for our involvement. One said to come back at Series A.

That is the lesson, and founders hate it. Those investors were not wrong about their own funds. They were answering a different question from the one Chesky thought he was asking.

Seven nos from seven people who do not fund your stage or your category is not information about your company. It is information about your list.

On a raise we cut the list hard and spend the time on warm paths into funds that have actually written cheques into this shape of company recently. A shorter, better-targeted list beats a longer one. We can change who hears the pitch and how it lands, not the answer.

How many investors on your list have funded your stage this year?
