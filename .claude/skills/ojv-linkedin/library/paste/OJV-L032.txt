You can still debate whether humanoid robots are useful. You cannot debate who is shipping them.

Counterpoint Research counted more than 22,000 humanoid robots shipped worldwide in the first half of 2026, up nearly 300% on a year earlier.

One Chinese company, AgiBot, shipped around 9,700 of them. That is a share of over 43%.

The top five makers took 86% of everything shipped.

Read from a boardroom in London or New York, 9,700 robots in six months sounds like a pilot programme. It is the wrong frame.

A pilot is a handful of units on a test cell. Thousands of units in six months means actuators, reducers, sensors and battery packs arriving at that rate, from suppliers who are already tooled for it.

That supplier base is the part worth seeing. The robot is only the visible end of it, and it is the end every Western headline photographs.

The question for a Western operator has moved. It is no longer whether this works. It is who will already be building at scale when it does.
