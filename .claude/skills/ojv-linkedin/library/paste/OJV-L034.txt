In 2022 Coca-Cola's biggest cola in India was not called Coca-Cola. It got there in three stages, and the first one was a mistake.

1/ Buy the local leader. In 1993 Coca-Cola bought the soft drinks portfolio of Parle's Ramesh Chauhan, and Thums Up came with it.

2/ Starve it. By Chauhan's account, Coca-Cola gave Thums Up no budget at all for at least the first two years, and cut supplies where it was strong. Business Today reported Rs 200 crore going behind Coca-Cola itself.

3/ Back it. Later in the decade, support for Thums Up started scaling up. In February 2022 chief executive James Quincey told investors that Thums Up had become a $1 billion brand in India. Nielsen data cited by Business Today put Pepsi second, behind Thums Up.

The unlock was the local brand. What Coca-Cola eventually stopped doing was insisting the global one had to win.

That is the uncomfortable version of localisation. Sometimes the asset you need in a market already belongs to it, and the hard part is letting it lead.
