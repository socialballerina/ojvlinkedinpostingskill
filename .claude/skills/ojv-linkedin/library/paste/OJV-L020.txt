148 investors turned Kathryn Minshew down before The Muse raised its seed round. Three things changed. The idea was not one of them.

She counted afterwards. "I had been turned down 148 times," she said on Masters of Scale.

The business stayed a careers site built around content, which was exactly what worried investors.

What changed was this.

The evidence. In her words, most investors were apathetic, but users were rabid fans, and the user feedback and data were what kept her going.

The objections got sharper. One investor told her to call back once The Muse hit 100,000 monthly active users. Another said she would have a hard time finding women who care about their careers.

The room. Y Combinator accepted them, which she called definitely a turning point. The seed round, from YC, 500 Startups and others, was announced in January 2013. In May 2015 The Muse raised a $10 million Series A from Aspect Ventures.

148 nos from investors who did not believe the buyer existed is not information about the company. It is information about the list.
