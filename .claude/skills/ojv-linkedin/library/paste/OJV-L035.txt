When Maggi came back to India in November 2015, it came back in 100 towns, not everywhere. Not by accident, but by design.

In June 2015 India's food regulator had banned Maggi noodles. In August the Bombay High Court lifted the ban, subject to fresh tests.

What Nestlé did next was sequencing.

→ Tests first. Maggi passed tests by three government-accredited laboratories before it went back on sale.
→ Narrow first. It returned in 100 towns through 300-odd distributors, and stayed out of eight states where it was still not allowed.
→ Online first. Snapdeal sold out the first batch of 60,000 welcome kits within five minutes.

By October 2016 Nestlé said its share of India's instant noodle market was back to 57%, against 75% before the crisis. In its 2023-24 annual report Nestlé India said it sold over six billion servings of Maggi in the year, making India the largest Nestlé market for Maggi worldwide.

The unlock was the order of operations. What Nestlé did not do was relaunch nationally into a market that had not finished deciding whether to trust it.
